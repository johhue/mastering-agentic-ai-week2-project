## Description
RemindMe helps you remember events that matter. You can set an event, choose how you want to be reminded, and decide whether other people should help remind you. Reminders can include phone alarms, social media posts, requests for a friend or coworker to call or text, and requests for a close relative to make personal contact.

This guide explains how to use RemindMe thoughtfully. The best reminder plan is one that gets your attention without surprising other people, exposing private information, or creating unnecessary stress.

## Getting Started
Create an account with a verified email address or phone number. Turn on notification permissions for your device. Check that your time zone is correct, especially before travel. If you plan to use phone alarms, keep your device charged and make sure sound, vibration, and focus settings allow RemindMe alerts.

## Creating an Event
To create an event, enter a clear title, date, time, and time zone. Add a description only if it will help you or your contacts. If the event is private, use a neutral title such as "Appointment," "Call," or "Deadline." You can add more detail in your own notes if needed, but avoid including sensitive information in messages that may go to other people.

Choose whether the event repeats. Review the event summary before saving. If the event has serious consequences, set earlier reminders so you have time to prepare, travel, or ask for help.

## Choosing Reminder Methods
Phone alarms are usually the first reminder method. You can choose when alarms start, whether they repeat, and whether you must acknowledge them. Acknowledgement helps RemindMe decide whether to escalate.

Social media reminders can ask your network to remind you. Use this option carefully. Review the message, audience, and platform before posting. Do not include private medical, legal, financial, or family details unless you are comfortable with others seeing them.

Friend or coworker reminders ask a person you choose to call or text you. This works best when you have already asked that person whether they are willing to help. Keep messages short and respectful.

## Adding Trusted Contacts
Add contacts by name, phone number, email, or supported messaging channel. Tell each person why you are adding them and what kind of reminders they may receive. A trusted contact can ignore, decline, or opt out of requests.

Choose the right person for the event. A coworker may be appropriate for a meeting reminder, while a family member may be better for a wake-up check before travel. Avoid adding people who may feel pressured, unsafe, or uncomfortable.

## Managing Privacy
You control what reminder content is shared. Before enabling social media or contact outreach, preview the exact message. If you do not want someone to know the reason for an event, use general wording such as "Please remind me to leave by 8:00 AM."

## Editing, Pausing, and Canceling
You can edit an event before it sends reminders. Changes may include time, title, channels, contacts, escalation steps, and recurrence. If you no longer need a reminder, cancel it. If you need a break from repeated events, pause the series rather than deleting it.

If a reminder has already been sent to a contact or posted publicly, canceling the event may not erase what others have already seen. Follow the platform or contact instructions to remove messages where possible.

## Best Practices
Use layered reminders for critical events. Set a quiet first alarm, a stronger second reminder, and human backup only when needed. Keep reminder messages simple. Confirm that trusted contacts are willing to help. Test your setup before relying on it. For emergencies, call local emergency services instead of relying on RemindMe.
